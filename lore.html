001 // OVERVIEW

The Bureau of International Defence was founded in 1976 by then Prime Minister James Callaghan and MI6 intelligence officer Jean Clark. Originally operating as a conventional British intelligence agency, BID handled national affairs and threats requiring specialized intelligence capabilities.

As intelligence warfare intensified across Europe and security breaches exposed vulnerabilities within existing agencies, BID was pushed into territory conventional intelligence could not safely occupy.

In 1984, the Bureau was publicly dissolved following a period of widespread infiltration and intelligence instability. The organization did not actually disappear.

Jean Clark gathered the remaining trusted personnel and rebuilt BID in secrecy. Over the following decades, the Bureau expanded from Britain to Europe and eventually across the world.

BID no longer operates where the law permits. It operates where the mission requires.

BLACK DIRECTIVE 17
OMEGA

Black Directive 17 is the legal foundation upon which modern BID operates.

The directive permits BID to conduct operations outside conventional legal and diplomatic frameworks when national or global stability is determined to be at risk.

Under the directive, BID possesses extraordinary operational freedom. Its personnel are protected from becoming conventional war criminals or international fugitives as a consequence of authorized operations.

The Director General maintains primary authority over BID operations.

Actions involving the destruction of evidence, manipulation of foreign governments, or other significant illegal activity require authorization from the Prime Minister of the United Kingdom.

AUTHORIZATION DOES NOT EQUAL ACCOUNTABILITY.

BLACK DIRECTIVE 17 REMAINS CLASSIFIED.
