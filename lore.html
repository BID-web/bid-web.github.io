001

ORIGIN

The Bureau of International Defence was established in 1976 as a British intelligence agency dedicated to protecting national security and maintaining global stability.

The Bureau was founded by then Prime Minister James Callaghan and Jean Clark, an intelligence officer within MI6. In its earliest years, BID operated as a conventional agency, handling matters primarily within the United Kingdom.

That role would not last.

"Some threats cannot be handled within the boundaries of conventional intelligence."
BID // FOUNDING DIRECTIVE

As intelligence warfare intensified throughout Europe and tensions with the Soviet Union escalated, serious security breaches began exposing weaknesses within existing intelligence structures.

BID was increasingly tasked with dealing with threats that conventional agencies could not safely approach.

002

THE DISAPPEARANCE

In 1984, a series of intelligence breaches and infiltration operations affected agencies throughout Europe. BID was among those compromised.

The Bureau faced a choice.

Rebuild publicly and risk exposing the organization again, or disappear completely.

"BID did not survive by rebuilding what had been lost. It survived by becoming something that could not be found."
INTERNAL HISTORICAL RECORD // 1984

Jean Clark gathered the remaining trusted personnel and reorganized BID in secrecy.

The organization was publicly dissolved. Its facilities were abandoned, its existence erased from official records, and its personnel scattered into new identities and positions.

To the public, BID was gone.

It wasn't.

003

THE RECONSTRUCTION

Following the 1984 dissolution, BID ceased operating as a conventional intelligence agency and began functioning as a covert organization outside the public intelligence structure.

Under Jean Clark's leadership, the Bureau rebuilt itself through compartmentalization, secrecy, and carefully selected personnel.

BID gradually expanded beyond British national affairs, establishing operations throughout Europe before eventually developing a worldwide intelligence network.

By 2005, BID possessed operational reach across the globe.

The organization that emerged was fundamentally different from the agency founded in 1976.

BID no longer needed recognition.

It needed results.

005

BLACK DIRECTIVE 17

BID's authority is derived from a classified contingency provision known as Black Directive 17.

BLACK DIRECTIVE 17
CLEARANCE // OMEGA

Black Directive 17 permits BID to conduct operations outside conventional legal and diplomatic frameworks when national or global stability is determined to be at risk.

Under the directive, BID possesses extraordinary operational freedom. Its personnel are protected from becoming conventional war criminals or international fugitives as a consequence of authorized operations.

The Director General maintains primary operational authority.

However, actions involving the destruction of evidence, manipulation of foreign governments, or other significant illegal activity require authorization from the Prime Minister of the United Kingdom.

AUTHORIZATION DOES NOT EQUAL ACCOUNTABILITY.

BLACK DIRECTIVE 17 REMAINS CLASSIFIED.

007

LEADERSHIP

BID is privately controlled, with the Director General holding primary authority over the Bureau's operations.

The British government, specifically the sitting Prime Minister, retains limited authority over matters falling under the highest levels of Black Directive 17.

FIRST DIRECTOR GENERAL // 1984 — 2009
JEAN CLARK
Founder of BID alongside James Callaghan and the architect of its transformation into a covert organization. Clark led the Bureau through its reconstruction and expansion before passing leadership to Evelyn Hart in 2009.
DIRECTOR GENERAL // 2009 — PRESENT
EVELYN HART
The current Director General of BID. Calm, controlled, and exceptionally perceptive, Hart maintains the Bureau's mission above personal considerations. Her authority is absolute within the limits of Black Directive 17.
008

THE UNKNOWN

Approximately 98% of personnel within other intelligence organizations are unaware that BID exists.

The few who are aware generally know only the surface. They know that something operates in the shadows. They know that it is ruthless. They know that it does not answer to conventional authority.

They do not know how large BID is.

They do not know where it operates.

And most importantly, they do not know what it is capable of.

The less the world knows about BID, the safer BID — and the world — remains.
